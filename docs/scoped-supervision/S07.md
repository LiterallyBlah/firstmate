## Protocol, as built

0. **Preflight (inside `dryRun` and `prepare`, before anything is frozen).** The whole prepared transaction is checked with the store's read-only `validate`: schema, every row rule, the task/host index and the pilot ceilings. That check also covers the rest of the run's worst-case budget (`migrationCost`):
   - each transfer: map, remove, acknowledge;
   - each hold: resolve, remove or drain;
   - prepared, barrier and commit;
   - 8 transactions of re-inventory headroom;
   - 1 transaction reserved for a release.

   It also checks the activation body: three rows plus one per imported attempt, against the 256-mutation limit. A cutover is capped at **64** affected deliveries. The dry run reports all of this as `capacity`, and a plan that could not finish is never frozen (B4).
1. **Arm** (`arm`). This writes `.scoped/armed.json` and snapshots the entries already queued. From then on, every gated send leaves a claim. A pre-arming entry can later be `uncertain`, never unsent. Unarmed, the gate writes nothing.
2. **Freeze** (`prepare`). Both of these are held while the target's state is captured: the gate lock, then the legacy writer's lock.
   - **Refusals, with no freeze:** a corrupt, quarantined, retired or recovery-receipt history; a replay-parity mismatch; a live in-flight send; a failed preflight; or a **missing, disabled, stale or unknown Needs-you ledger**, or one with a quarantined row that may belong to the plan (B3).
   - **On success:** `freeze.json` is published. From then on, the legacy writer and every gated delivery path refuse the plan.
3. **Prepared** (one scoped transaction; its request ID is the migration ID). It contains:
   - the scope (`transferring`) and the prepared backend;
   - the byte-exact `workflow-import`;
   - the run tasks;
   - the receipt, with its imported index, capacity, dependents, launch records and projection preimage;
   - one disposition per delivery.

   `prepared.json` follows. While the backend is `prepared`, neither backend can write.
4. **Reinventory** (run by `reconcile`, `resolveHold` and `barrier`; also callable directly). Anything new for the frozen plan becomes a **hold** disposition: watch output that names it after the freeze, a stray identity note from a non-compliant writer, or a crashed send claim not yet named by any disposition. It is never a terminal stray (B1, B2).
5. **Reconcile.**
   - **Transfer facts:** the `legacy.fact` outbox mapping is committed first. The exact old entry is then archived under `.scoped/<plan>/removed/`, and only then removed by ID and payload hash.
   - **A payload mismatch** removes nothing and becomes a hold.
   - **Drains** are accounted once the recorded old owner's send has a receipt. A row a full watch queue dropped is recorded `absent` and is never re-sent.
6. **Resolve a hold** (`resolveHold`). This is an explicit move to `already-handled` or `drain-legacy`. It needs an evidence reference and its SHA-256 digest, and a named owner (below). It never sends. The evidence is cooperative attestation, not proof.
7. **Barrier.** Everything is verified again: the freeze, the source fingerprint, no unaccounted disposition, and no open claim. A claim that a disposition names (as `uncertain`) counts as accounted.
8. **Commit.** `committed.json` is published first. Then one transaction commits the migration, activates the backend and the scope, and **imports every legacy attempt as a scoped attempt row** (`importedFrom`). The rows carry worker identity, cumulative spend, the original notification owner, and `reported` or `unknown` status; no launch effect is invented. S06's `hasScopedWorker`, and S08's attempt-keyed guard after the merge, therefore recognize migrated workers (M1). The gate also refuses legacy board steer, interrupt, end and relaunch for a frozen or migrated plan's workers, and refuses its item discussions.
9. **Acknowledge.** The bound owner handles each transferred fact through the existing typed path: an `ack` command result, then `acknowledge`. `recordAcknowledgements` copies that result onto the disposition.
10. **Report.** `report().complete` is true only when all of these hold (B5):
    - the cutover has committed;
    - every transfer has its owner's result;
    - no hold remains;
    - no worker callback is unaccounted;
    - no open claim for the plan remains.

    `legacyDeliveryPossible` lists whatever is still open. `originalRouteKept` names the one stated exclusion: watch output that only mentions the plan keeps its original route until S08/S10 classify its source.

**Release** (`abort`) is supported from prepared or barrier, before the committed sidecar exists:
1. One transaction marks the migration `aborted`, with its actor, reason and restored entries, and retires the backend and scope.
2. Every archived entry is restored exactly, and only where its identity is absent.
3. `aborted.json` is published.

The legacy writer, the gate and `legacyRoute` then hand the plan back. The release sends nothing itself. A restored informational note can reach General again under its original ID; that is the allowed informational reoffer. A released plan is not migrated again in the same journal: that needs a new design. After commit, recovery is forward only, by holding or by transfer at a higher epoch.

`resume` finishes whatever stage a crash interrupted: the prepared commit, the prepared sidecar, activation, or a release that has not finished restoring. A changed source or inventory stays held until it is released.

## Dispositions, owners and procedures

| Source | Identity kept | Route | Owner and step |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plan captain action | Action and note ID; handled/pending keys; `notified` as an observation only | `already-handled` or `transfer-fact` (one stable consequence) | Automatic in `reconcile`. The scoped owner acknowledges. |
| Burst note for the plan's worker | Note ID and the whole persisted payload | `transfer-fact` | Automatic; the scoped owner acknowledges. On a payload mismatch it becomes a hold, which the main first mate resolves. |
| Decision submission | Request, revision, submission and message ID; payload hash; outcome; claim | `hold` (imperative) | **The captain only** (`captain-ui`): `already-handled` with the old owner's handling evidence, or `drain-legacy` (the old owner keeps it). Never re-sent or re-answered. |
| Watch row naming the plan (before or after the freeze) | Exact name, run, kind and payload hash | `hold` (opaque) | The main first mate or the captain: `drain-legacy` lets General receive it, or `already-handled`. |
| Worker callback / uncertain launch | Attempt, agent and launch file | `hold` | The main first mate: once the worker is quiescent and its finish is recorded, `drain-legacy` or `already-handled` with that evidence. |
| Crashed send claim | Claim ID and items | `hold` | The main first mate, with evidence of what arrived. It is never treated as unsent. |
| Damaged sidecar (not a disposition) | Directory and readable stages | Blocks only what cannot be proved unrelated | The main first mate inspects it, preserves the `.scoped/` directory, and reports to the captain. The record is never repaired by tool. If the plan never committed, release it. |

When no hold can be resolved honestly, the main first mate releases the cutover (`abort`) with a reason. The plan stays with General.

## Limits (stated, not solved)

- **Cooperative, in-process.** The gate and the sidecar bind compatible code only. A build without them must not be running. The runbook's single-build check (`paseo plugin ls --json`) is the precondition; the code cannot verify it.
- **Attestation.** Hold resolutions are cooperative attestations, named by actor kind. They are not authenticated captain identity.
- **No U2.** A worker's native callback still goes to its original creator. S07 only refuses to call the cutover complete until each one is accounted.
- **No bulk migration.** No archive, unknown-host adoption, RPC, timer or start-up wiring. One scope per cutover, up to 64 deliveries. A released plan is not migrated again in the same journal.
- **Watch mentions are a clue only.** Matching uses whole words, case-insensitively. Generic step names (shorter than 10 characters with no separator or digit) are not clues. A mention can hold; it never chooses an owner.
